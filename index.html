A tribute

Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

The boy from Rameswaram who taught a billion people to look up.

15 OCT 1931—27 JUL 2015

His story

From a boat-builder's home to the nation's highest office

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born in Rameswaram, a small island town in Tamil Nadu. His father owned a boat and led prayers at the local mosque. The family had little money, so young Kalam delivered newspapers before school to help with expenses. Watching birds glide above the shoreline sparked a curiosity about flight that never left him.

He studied physics at St. Joseph's College in Tiruchirappalli, then aeronautical engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology. He joined India's defence research establishment and later moved to the Indian Space Research Organisation. There he led the project that put the Rohini satellite into orbit on India's own launch vehicle in 1980.

In the decades that followed he guided the nation's missile programme and helped coordinate the Pokhran-II tests of 1998. People began calling him the Missile Man of India, though he preferred to be remembered as a teacher. He served as the eleventh President of India from 2002 to 2007 and earned the affection of ordinary citizens, which is why many still call him the People's President.

After leaving office he went back to the classroom. He lectured to students across the country, urging them to think big and work hard. He was giving one of those lectures at IIM Shillong when he passed away in 2015. It was a fitting end for a man who believed his best work was done in front of young minds.

Key achievements

A life in milestones

  1. 1931

    Born in Rameswaram

    Grows up beside the sea in a modest, close-knit family.

  2. 1958

    Joins defence research

    Begins his career as a scientist after graduating in aeronautical engineering.

  3. 1980

    Rohini reaches orbit

    As project director, he sees India's first indigenous satellite launch vehicle succeed.

  4. 1997

    Bharat Ratna

    Receives India's highest civilian honour for his scientific contribution.

  5. 1998

    Pokhran-II

    Serves as chief project coordinator for the nuclear tests alongside Dr. R. Chidambaram.

  6. 1999

    Wings of Fire

    Publishes his autobiography with Arun Tiwari, which inspires readers across generations.

  7. 2002

    11th President of India

    Takes office on 25 July and serves a full five-year term.

  8. 2015

    A teacher to the end

    Passes away on 27 July while lecturing students at IIM Shillong.

Dream, dream, dream. Dreams transform into thoughts, and thoughts result in action.

— Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

Remembered

He left behind rockets, books and a generation of students who believe the sky is a starting line. Every time a child in a small town decides to dream bigger, his work continues.
